Framework

The official Workspace-Bench framework figure summarizes how role-specific workspaces, benchmark tasks, agent execution, and capability-oriented evaluation fit together.

What Workspace-Bench Measures

The benchmark focuses on workspace learning: the ability to identify explicit and implicit file dependencies, integrate context across artifacts, choose the right actions, and produce dependable deliverables in large workspaces.

File Dependency Discovery

Finds relevant files even when the critical signal is not exposed at the task root.

Cross-file Reasoning

Combines evidence across notes, data tables, code, policy documents, and metadata.

Adaptive Decision Making

Chooses whether to inspect, modify, summarize, validate, or preserve artifacts based on workspace state.

Deliverable Quality

Scores the final artifact against detailed rubrics instead of only a single pass or fail condition.

Current Performance Gap

Workspace-Bench exposes a large gap between human performance and the best AI agents. The gap grows in profiles with more hidden dependencies, denser file graphs, and stricter rubric requirements.

Benchmark Highlights

Workspace-Bench includes both the full benchmark and a cost-reduced lite split for repeatable evaluation, with real workspaces spanning documents, spreadsheets, code, media, metadata, and archives.

Full Benchmark

388 tasks with large workspaces, multi-step evidence gathering, and 7,399 rubrics for comprehensive evaluation.

Lite Benchmark

100 tasks designed to preserve representative complexity while reducing evaluation cost by approximately 70%.

Benchmark Breadth

5 worker profiles, 74 file types, and up to 20GB per workspace make context management part of the task, not a side condition.

Evaluation Scope

The paper reports results across 4 agent harnesses and 7 foundation models, with humans retained as a reference point.
